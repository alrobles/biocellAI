\begin{abstract}
Single-cell models increasingly align expression profiles with biological
text, but whether the text contributes anything beyond the supervision used
to write it is rarely measured. We evaluate this directly with an
arm matrix that equalizes cells, donor splits, encoder capacity, update
budget and identity-label access: a supervised MLP trained with a direct
donor-pathology head (B2), non-text readouts (composition B0;
pseudobulk-PCA B1), non-semantic contrastive references (T0 one-hot;
T1 random prototypes), and two text-grounded contrastive arms (T2 marker
captions; T3 PubMed/RAG captions), plus a permuted-endpoint null (N1)
and a permuted caption-map null (N2). Exploratory runs (seeds 0--2) were
frozen before a confirmation phase on fresh splits (seeds 3--5 of SEA-AD
MTG and ROSMAP, plus a SEA-AD PFC robustness leg), 100 shared
donor-permutations per seed, donor-clustered bootstrap intervals, and
pre-registered gates. The decisive comparison for a semantic benefit ---
text arms versus the equally endpoint-blind T1 reference --- shows no
detectable advantage: confirmatory paired $\Delta\rho$ is $+0.041$
(T2$-$T1) and $+0.007$ (T3$-$T1) on the primary cohort, both CIs
crossing zero. Against direct endpoint supervision the text arms lose:
confirmatory SEA-AD MTG $\rho$ was 0.454 (B2) versus 0.261 (T2) and
0.228 (T3), and ROSMAP text arms were significantly \emph{worse} than
B2 under paired donor-level tests. In contrast, the source-fitted B2
predictor passed strict cross-cohort transfer in both registered
directions (ROSMAP$\leftrightarrow$SEA-AD MTG, $\rho=0.40$--$0.53$ over
all seeds, gate $\rho\geq0.35$) and in an MTG$\to$PFC same-study
robustness check ($\rho\approx0.77$, donors overlapping). An
endpoint-supervision null --- encoder retrained on permuted pathology
labels, evaluated honestly --- retains $\rho\approx0.3$--$0.4$,
quantifying how much pathology signal the identity/composition channel
carries without endpoint supervision; a complementary readout-permuted
null on B2's frozen features centers at zero, verifying the evaluation
machinery. Marker-gene recovery against external references failed its
gate structurally (only 13--33\% of reference genes are evaluable in a
2{,}000-HVG space). We conclude that under this endpoint and donor
scale, contrastive text grounding provides no detectable benefit over a
non-semantic reference, and falls short of direct endpoint supervision,
while donor-level pathology signal is real and transferable. This is a
bounded negative result for the registered comparison, not a claim that
biological language cannot help under different designs.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Single-cell foundation models --- scGPT \citep{scgpt}, Geneformer
\citep{geneformer}, scFoundation \citep{scfoundation} and successors ---
learn cell representations from expression profiles alone. A parallel line
of work argues that grounding those representations in biological language
unlocks generalization that molecular data alone cannot provide:
CellWhisperer aligns transcriptomes with AI-curated textual annotations
\citep{cellwhisperer}, C2S-Scale renders expression profiles as ``cell
sentences'' inside a general-purpose LLM \citep{c2sscale}, and multimodal
efforts increasingly treat text as a first-class modality.

The motivating question --- \emph{does language grounding improve cell
representations?} --- is harder to answer than it looks, because
``grounding'' bundles at least three distinct mechanisms: (i) text can
carry \emph{label-discriminative structure} (captions derived from
cell-type labels); (ii) text can carry \emph{external biological
knowledge} (function, lineage, marker semantics); and (iii) text can act
as a \emph{representation bottleneck} that smooths per-cell measurement
noise. Published systems typically report end-to-end performance and leave
the decomposition implicit --- and typically compare text arms against
weaker supervision rather than an equally strong non-text baseline.

This paper reports a deliberately controlled decomposition. Earlier
exploratory work in this project (protocol v1) appeared to show that
caption-grounded embeddings outperform expression-only baselines on
donor-level pathology prediction. A methodological audit then identified
conflation mechanisms in that record: repeated preprocessing on the same
splits, reuse of fold-derived captions at evaluation, transductive PCA,
target-label orientation in transfer analyses, and non-equivalent input
protocols in external comparisons. Protocol v2 (ADR-011) freezes data
contracts, makes every transformation train-only, pits each training
signal against an equivalent-supervision control, and evaluates
statistically at the donor level --- the unit of replication for any
donor-held-out claim. Exploratory v2 runs were frozen before a
confirmation phase on never-inspected splits.

Our contributions are empirical and methodological:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item A reproducible donor-held-out benchmark in which cell identity
    and donor pathology are evaluated under a nine-arm
    controlled-supervision matrix, verified data lineage, shared
    permutation nulls and donor-clustered inference
    (Section~\ref{sec:methods}).
  \item A bounded \emph{negative result}: contrastive biological-text
    alignment (marker captions or retrieved PubMed text) shows no
    detectable benefit over equally endpoint-blind non-semantic
    references, and loses to direct endpoint supervision, in either
    exploratory or confirmatory phases (Section~\ref{sec:pathology}).
  \item A \emph{positive, confirmed result}: a supervised MLP trained on
    one cohort predicts donor pathology on another cohort with a
    strictly source-fitted predictor --- encoder, normalizations, axis
    and readout all frozen --- in both registered directions and all
    seeds (Section~\ref{sec:transfer}).
  \item Quantification of a subtle confound: an endpoint-supervision
    null --- encoder retrained on permuted pathology labels, identity
    head intact, honest readout --- retains $\rho\approx0.3$--$0.4$,
    decomposing how much apparent pathology signal the non-endpoint
    (identity/composition) channels carry
    (Section~\ref{sec:nulls}).
  \item A structural marker-recovery audit showing the pre-registered
    retrieval gate was unreachable under the evaluation space, with the
    attainable ceiling reported rather than the gate redefined
    post hoc (Section~\ref{sec:markers}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related work}

\paragraph{Expression-only single-cell foundation models.}
scGPT \citep{scgpt}, Geneformer \citep{geneformer}, scFoundation
\citep{scfoundation}, and UCE \citep{uce} learn representations from
expression alone; UCE additionally targets cross-species universality via
protein-embedding gene tokens. Independent zero-shot evaluations find
these models underperform simple baselines \citep{kedzierska,scfm_limits}:
\citet{kedzierska} show Geneformer and scGPT lose to HVG selection plus
Harmony/scVI on cell-type clustering, and \citet{scfm_limits} report
Seurat v5 beating all three on classification. The burden of proof for
any added modality is therefore high --- motivating the controlled,
leakage-aware protocol used here.

\paragraph{Language-grounded cell models.}
LangCell \citep{langcell} established contrastive cell--text pretraining
on metadata-derived captions; CellWhisperer \citep{cellwhisperer} scales
CLIP-style alignment to $\sim$1M cells with AI-curated descriptions;
C2S-Scale \citep{c2sscale} and Cell2Sentence \citep{cell2sentence}
serialize expression as ``cell sentences'' inside general LLMs. None of
these decompose which part of the text carries the transferable signal
under matched supervision --- our central question.

\paragraph{Knowledge-grounded approaches.}
At the gene level, GenePT \citep{genept} and scGenePT \citep{scgenept}
show text-derived gene embeddings are additive but not sufficient alone.
Ontology-structured supervision appears in OnClass \citep{onclass},
scCello \citep{sccello}, and KCFM \citep{kcfm}; retrieval-based
annotation in scRAG \citep{scrag} and scHilda \citep{schilda}. These
ground in \emph{external} knowledge --- the direction our results leave
open --- but target annotation rather than donor-level transfer.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Data and frozen splits}
\label{sec:data}

Three cohorts are used (Table~\ref{tab:data}). Tabula Sapiens blood
\citep{tabulasapiens} (accessed via CZ CELLxGENE Census
\citep{cellxgene}) provides a cell-\emph{identity} task (9 donors, 16
cell types). The SEA-AD Alzheimer's atlas provides donor-\emph{pathology}
tasks: a primary middle temporal gyrus cohort (MTG; 84 donors) and a
prefrontal-cortex leg (PFC; 80 donors) used only for confirmation and
same-study transfer robustness; the endpoint is the continuous cortical
pathology score (CPS\_Global), a donor-level summary of quantitative
neuropathology, not a longitudinal observation. The open ROSMAP
multiregion release \citep{liu2025rosmap} (111 donors) provides an
external pathology cohort whose public matrix carries a coarse
three-level ordinal pathology label (nonAD $<$ earlyAD $<$ lateAD);
we pre-registered this asymmetry against continuous CPS.

Each cohort is reduced to a 2{,}000-gene highly-variable space selected
on \emph{training donors only}, normalized per cell (library size to
$10^4$, $\log_1p$), and split by \emph{whole donors} ($\sim$25\% held
out). Fold artifacts record source hashes, code hashes, donor lists and
the resulting matrix hash; the inference pipeline re-verifies fold
SHA-256 values before evaluating gates (integrity gate,
Section~\ref{sec:stats}). Exploratory folds use seeds 0--2;
confirmatory folds use never-inspected seeds 3--5 (MTG, ROSMAP) and
0--2 for the disjoint PFC cell population. All results below report
predictions on held-out donors.

\subsection{Arm matrix: what is equalized, what is not}
\label{sec:arms}

All arms see the same cells, the same donor split, the same encoder
capacity (a four-layer MLP producing a 64-d cell embedding), the same
update budget and the same
identity-label access; only the \emph{training signal} differs.
Critically, the arms differ in \emph{when and how} the pathology endpoint
is visible: B2 optimizes it directly during representation learning,
whereas the contrastive arms (T0--T3) never see it until the frozen
donor-level ridge readout.

The matrix therefore supports two distinct comparisons: (a)
\emph{semantic benefit at equal endpoint access} --- text arms versus
the equally endpoint-blind references T0/T1, the clean test of whether
caption biology adds anything; and (b) \emph{contrastive text versus
direct endpoint supervision} --- text arms versus B2, which jointly
varies text use and endpoint supervision form. We report both
explicitly rather than calling the matrix ``equivalent supervision''
for the endpoint; a text arm \emph{with} a B2-style endpoint head would
isolate a third contrast and is noted as future work.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{B0} donor composition ($\pm$ covariates) $\to$ ridge:
    no encoder, tests how far cell-type proportions alone carry the
    donor endpoint.
  \item \textbf{B1} pseudobulk expression $\to$ train-fit PCA $\to$
    ridge: the standard non-encoder baseline.
  \item \textbf{B2} supervised MLP with two heads: cell-type
    cross-entropy plus a donor-pathology regression head --- the
    non-text reference with equal capacity and label access.
  \item \textbf{T0} contrastive alignment vs.\ \emph{one-hot} class
    prototypes: identity structure without semantics.
  \item \textbf{T1} contrastive alignment vs.\ \emph{fixed random}
    prototypes: contrastive geometry without biology.
  \item \textbf{T2} contrastive alignment vs.\ marker-list captions
    (train-donor Wilcoxon markers, frozen at fold creation).
  \item \textbf{T3} contrastive alignment vs.\ PubMed/RAG-derived
    captions --- retrieved-literature text rather than engineered
    prose.
  \item \textbf{N1} B2 with donor$\to$pathology labels permuted within
    training donors: a supervision null that preserves donor
    structure.
  \item \textbf{N2} the cohort's stronger text arm (T3 for SEA-AD, T2
    for ROSMAP) with the class$\to$caption map permuted: a semantics
    null that preserves text format.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Caption construction.}
T2 captions are generated per fold: Wilcoxon differential expression on
\emph{training donors only} yields the top-8 markers per class, rendered
in a fixed template (``\emph{<class>}: a cell type defined by elevated
expression of \emph{<genes>}''); genes used in captions are logged so
downstream evaluations can exclude them. T3 captions come from a
pre-registered per-cohort corpus
(\texttt{type\_desc\_pubmed\_rag\_markers\_alias\_*}): PubMed titles and
abstracts retrieved per class over marker-gene queries with HGNC alias
expansion, reranked by marker coverage and BM25, and concatenated
verbatim (no LLM synthesis in this arm); classes missing from the
corpus fall back to a name-only caption, recorded in the provenance
file. Each fold's caption bank is frozen at fold creation,
hashed, and stored with its provenance manifest; N2's permuted
class$\to$caption map uses the same bank.

Donor pathology is read out by a ridge fit on train-donor pooled
embeddings and evaluated as Spearman $\rho$ on held-out donors;
per-donor predictions are exported for paired inference. Cell identity
is read out by a linear probe (and, for contrastive arms, zero-shot
nearest-prototype scoring) and evaluated as macro-F1 on held-out cells.

\subsection{Statistical protocol and gates}
\label{sec:stats}

Inference is performed on \emph{paired} per-donor predictions, not on
per-seed aggregate metrics:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Paired differences.} For each arm comparison, $\Delta\rho$
    is computed on donors shared across folds. Confidence intervals come
    from 2{,}000 bootstrap replicates that \emph{cluster by donor across
    seeds} --- resampling donors rather than seed$\times$donor cells,
    because the same donor can appear in several folds' test sets.
  \item \textbf{Multiplicity.} Primary comparisons (T2/T3 vs.\ B2 and
    vs.\ T1 on the primary cohort, SEA-AD MTG) use empirical
    permutation p-values; all secondary comparisons carry
    Benjamini--Hochberg q-values.
  \item \textbf{Permutation nulls (two complementary designs).} 100
    shared donor$\to$label permutation maps per seed (300 per cohort)
    permute labels \emph{among training donors only} and are applied to
    every arm. (i) \emph{Readout null}: for B0/B1/T0--T3/N2, features
    are frozen and the donor ridge is fit on the permuted target ---
    a full label null expected to center at zero. (ii)
    \emph{Endpoint-supervision null}: for B2, the encoder is
    \emph{retrained} with permuted pathology labels (identity head
    intact; an N1 replicate) and evaluated with a true-label ridge ---
    this isolates how much endpoint signal the non-endpoint channels
    carry, and is not expected to reach zero if composition or identity
    structure carries pathology information. A third check, added in
    revision, permutes the readout target over the \emph{frozen} B2
    encoder's features to verify the machinery on B2 specifically.
    Observed $\rho$ is compared against the appropriate null's 95th
    percentile.
  \item \textbf{Pre-registered gates} (evaluated once per phase,
    automatically, from artifacts): integrity (identical fold hashes),
    improvement ($\Delta\rho\geq0.05$ over B2 with CI lower bound $>0$),
    semantics (beat T1 \emph{and} exceed the shared null q95),
    historical-threshold G5b ($\rho\geq0.5$), transfer (strict $\rho\geq
    0.35$), composition-adjusted improvement, and a
    negative-result-validity check that the full matrix ran with correct
    provenance.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Strict cross-cohort transfer}
\label{sec:transfer_methods}

Transfer is \emph{source-fitted}: the donor-level predictor (encoder
weights, gene space, train-fit PCA/ridge, label axis and sign) is frozen
on the source cohort; the target cohort contributes only cells
re-quantified into the source gene space, and no target labels are
touched. The gate is $\rho\geq0.35$ over all target donors. A
\emph{calibrated refit} variant (readout refit on target train donors,
evaluated on target test donors) is reported separately as an
orientation analysis and never counted toward the strict gate.
MTG$\to$PFC transfer is additionally flagged as a same-study robustness
check: PFC and MTG donors overlap.

\subsection{Marker-recovery evaluation}
\label{sec:marker_methods}

To probe whether biologically meaningful genes drive the caption
machinery, we rank genes by train-only Wilcoxon differential expression,
\emph{exclude} the genes injected into that fold's captions
(non-circular evaluation), and score the next top-10 against external
PanglaoDB marker sets \citep{panglaodb} mapped to each cohort's cell-type
vocabulary (P@10 macro over evaluable types). The pre-registered gate
was P@10 $\geq 0.6$. We additionally report the \emph{attainable
ceiling}: the best achievable P@10 given how many reference genes exist
inside the 2{,}000-HVG evaluation space.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Integrity and identity task}
\label{sec:identity}

Fold-hash verification passed for all runs in both phases
(\texttt{integrity: PASS}); every reported number traces to a fold whose
source, code and donor lists are manifest-recorded.

All encoder arms learn cell identity on held-out donors
(Table~\ref{tab:identity}). On the brain cohorts every encoder arm
reaches probe macro-F1 0.86--0.99 (B2 best: 0.95--0.99); on blood

contrastive arms at 0.49--0.55 probe / 0.51--0.61 zero-shot. Identity is
therefore a solved subproblem at this scale and not the discriminating
axis between arms; the decisive comparisons are on donor pathology.

\subsection{No detectable benefit of text grounding on donor pathology}
\label{sec:pathology}

Figure~\ref{fig:arms} and Table~\ref{tab:rho} report donor-level
pathology $\rho$ across arms and phases. Three observations recur in
both phases. First, the strongest signals come from \emph{non-text}
supervision: B2 and the pseudobulk B1 lead on every pathology cohort.
Second, the text arms T2 and T3 sit at or below the non-semantic
references T0/T1 on the primary cohort and below them on ROSMAP; they
never approach B2. Third, B0 --- donor composition alone --- carries
non-trivial signal (confirmatory $\rho=0.24$--$0.32$), consistent with
the composition confound documented in Section~\ref{sec:nulls}.

Paired donor-level inference (Figure~\ref{fig:paired}) separates the two
registered comparisons. \emph{Semantic benefit at equal endpoint access}
(text vs.\ the equally endpoint-blind T1): on the primary cohort,
$\Delta\rho$(T2$-$T1) $= +0.041$ (95\% CI $-0.036$ to $0.132$) and
$\Delta\rho$(T3$-$T1) $= +0.007$ ($-0.125$ to $0.162$) ---
statistically indistinguishable from prototypes containing no biology;
at 84--111 donors the CIs bound the plausible benefit below roughly
$\pm0.15$, so modest advantages cannot be excluded but no benefit is
detected. \emph{Contrastive text vs.\ direct endpoint supervision} (text
vs.\ B2): confirmatory $\Delta\rho$(T2$-$B2) $= -0.192$ ($-0.487$ to
$0.109$) and $\Delta\rho$(T3$-$B2) $= -0.226$ ($-0.491$ to $0.047$) on
MTG; on ROSMAP the deficit is significant in the exploratory phase
($\Delta\rho \approx -0.27$ to $-0.38$, FDR $q<0.05$) and replicates in
sign under confirmation. Because B2 optimizes the endpoint during
representation learning and the text arms do not, this second comparison
is confounded by supervision form --- it bounds what contrastive text
achieves relative to the strongest available alternative, not a pure
text effect.

\subsection{Permutation nulls quantify non-semantic signal}
\label{sec:nulls}

Figure~\ref{fig:null} overlays each arm's observed confirmatory $\rho$
on its shared permutation-null distribution; the two null designs
(Section~\ref{sec:stats}) answer different questions and must be read
separately.

\emph{Readout nulls (features frozen, ridge target permuted).} For every
non-B2 arm these center at zero (grand means $|{\mu}|\leq0.01$ across
600+ replicates per arm per cohort), verifying that the evaluation
machinery itself produces no signal under label destruction. Observed
text-arm $\rho$ (0.19--0.29) nonetheless lies \emph{below} the q95 of
their own readout-permuted nulls (0.32--0.46) --- the readout is noisy
enough at this donor scale that chance alignments of frozen features
with permuted labels can exceed the observed values; the semantics gate
fails outright. A verification added in revision permutes the ridge
target over the \emph{frozen} B2 encoder's features on the same shared
maps: the resulting null centers at zero (means $-0.01$ to $+0.02$,
q95 $0.28$--$0.41$ over 300 permutations per cohort), consistent with
all other readout nulls --- the machinery is sound on B2's features
too.

\emph{Endpoint-supervision null (B2 retrained on permuted endpoint,
true-label readout; N1 replicate).} This null is deliberately different:
it asks how much endpoint signal an encoder \emph{trained without
access to the true endpoint} still supports when evaluated honestly.
It does not collapse to zero --- mean $\rho\approx0.29$ (ROSMAP), 0.31
(MTG), 0.16 (PFC), q95 0.39--0.59 --- because the intact identity head
preserves cell-type structure, and donor-level pooling of that
structure (composition plus donor-associated expression) correlates
with pathology. The in-run N1 arm shows the same effect
($\rho=0.36$--$0.41$ on ROSMAP/MTG). The correct reading is therefore
not ``donor identity memorization fakes pathology'' but a measured
decomposition: roughly two-thirds of B2's observed $\rho$ is reachable
through non-endpoint channels alone, and the endpoint head adds the
remainder ($\approx0.1$--$0.15$). The cross-cohort strict transfer
result (Section~\ref{sec:transfer}) is the stronger evidence that this
remainder captures portable biology rather than within-cohort donor
structure.

\subsection{Strict source-fitted transfer replicates across cohorts}
\label{sec:transfer}

The positive claim that survives confirmation is B2 strict transfer
(Figure~\ref{fig:transfer}, Table~\ref{tab:transfer}). With predictor,
axis and sign entirely frozen on the source cohort, B2 trained on
ROSMAP predicts SEA-AD MTG donor CPS at $\rho=0.46$--$0.50$
(exploratory seeds 0--2) and $\rho=0.47$--$0.53$ (confirmatory seeds
3--5); the reverse direction reaches $\rho=0.44$--$0.50$ and
$0.40$--$0.50$ respectively --- every seed above the pre-registered
$\rho\geq0.35$ gate, in both phases. This is a portable,
non-cohort-specific pathology signal learned without text.

Two honest boundaries. First, MTG$\to$PFC transfer is much higher
($\rho=0.76$--$0.79$, all confirmatory seeds) but shares donors with
the source cohort; we report it as a same-study regional robustness
check, not independent replication. Second, the text arms do
\emph{not} transfer (strict $\rho$ near or below zero for T0--T3; see
generated table), and B1 transfers inconsistently (0.26--0.38, below

\subsection{Marker recovery: a structural gate failure}
\label{sec:markers}

Against external PanglaoDB references, fold-derived non-circular marker
rankings recover P@10 of only 0.019 (blood), 0.054 (MTG), 0.020
(ROSMAP), 0.024 (PFC) --- far below the pre-registered $\geq0.6$ gate.
The failure is partly structural: only 13--33\% of external reference
genes exist inside the 2{,}000-HVG evaluation space, so the maximum
achievable P@10 is 0.16--0.38 --- below the gate on every cohort. We
report the gate as failed rather than redefining it, with the ceiling
documented. Within the attainable range recovery is still weak: brain
PanglaoDB references collapse many excitatory subtypes into generic
categories, while fold-derived markers are subtype-specific. The
caption pipeline's genes are thus poor external-marker retrievers ---
consistent with the pathology results, captions here act as a
supervision format, not a knowledge channel.

\subsection{The exploratory record, superseded}
\label{sec:historical}

For provenance: protocol-v1 exploratory runs had reported apparent
text-grounding advantages on the same question (caption-grounded donor
embeddings with CPS $\rho\approx0.60$, cross-cohort transfer, and
external-marker retrieval). The audit that motivated ADR-011 showed
those estimates conflated the caption channel with (i) repeated
preprocessing on the same splits, (ii) evaluation against fold-derived
captions whose genes were selected on the same data, (iii) transductive
PCA fit before the donor split, and (iv) transfer analyses that used
target labels for orientation. Under the corrected pipeline --- frozen
splits, train-only transforms, equivalent-supervision controls,
strictly source-fitted transfer, donor-level inference --- the text
advantage does not reproduce, while a corrected version of the transfer
result does (Section~\ref{sec:transfer}). We retain the historical
numbers only in the repository reports, not as evidence here.

\section{Discussion}

\paragraph{What the negative result means --- and does not mean.}
The cleanest question the matrix answers is: does aligning a cell
encoder to biological captions add anything over equally
endpoint-blind, non-semantic contrastive references? The answer,
replicated across two independent cohorts and two phases on
never-inspected splits, is that we detect none --- text arms are
statistically indistinguishable from T1, with CIs that bound any
benefit to below roughly $\pm0.15$ in $\Delta\rho$. A second,
confounded-by-design comparison shows contrastive text loses to direct
endpoint supervision (B2) and to pseudobulk averaging. This is not a
claim that language is useless for single-cell modeling, nor that the
text arms could not be made stronger: it bounds the benefit of
\emph{this} grounding procedure on \emph{this} endpoint at this donor
scale. Designs that differ in task (annotation, retrieval), supervision
asymmetry (text used to \emph{reduce} label requirements), endpoint
access during encoder training (a text arm with a B2-style pathology
head, which our matrix deliberately leaves untested), or scale could
still show benefits --- the contribution here is a protocol capable of
detecting them cleanly.

\paragraph{Why supervised and pseudobulk signals win.}
B2's advantage plausibly reflects direct optimization of the endpoint:
the donor-pathology regression head shapes the pooled representation
toward exactly the quantity being read out, whereas contrastive text
alignment optimizes a surrogate objective whose information content is
bounded by caption quality and register. B1 matching B2 on ROSMAP
($\rho\approx0.50$ vs.\ 0.45) further shows that simple expression
averaging captures most of what a coarse ordinal endpoint contains ---
the added value of any encoder, grounded or not, is small when the
phenotype is driven by compositional shifts.

\paragraph{The residual-signal confound.}
That an encoder trained with a \emph{permuted} endpoint still supports
$\rho\approx0.3$--$0.4$ under honest evaluation (endpoint-supervision
null; N1 replicates it) is the most consequential methodological
observation, and it is worth being precise about what it does not
prove. Because the identity head remains intact during null training,
the retained signal cannot be attributed to donor bookkeeping alone:
it measures everything the non-endpoint channels carry --- cell-type
composition, donor-associated expression, and any residual identity
memorization pooled at the donor level. The complementary readout-perm
nulls (which destroy labels only at evaluation and do center at zero,
including on B2's own frozen features) rule out an artifact of the
readout machinery. Two implications follow. First, donor-level
evaluations need both nulls: raw correlations overstate every arm, and
retrain-style nulls must be interpreted as channel decompositions, not
sanity checks expected to reach zero. Second, our composition gate was
registered as \texttt{NOT\_EVALUABLE} in the confirmation phase
(covariate/residual block absent from confirmation runs); we report the
\emph{presence} of residual signal but claim no composition-adjusted
improvement for any arm.

\paragraph{What strict transfer does and does not establish.}
The source-fitted B2 transfer is the strongest positive evidence in the
study: a pathology readout trained on ROSMAP donors ranks MTG donors
correctly across every seed of both phases, and vice versa, without
touching target labels. Because no target fitting occurs, this is
portable signal rather than within-cohort donor-structure memorization
--- the answer to the confound above. However, both cohorts index
Alzheimer's-related pathology in post-mortem cortex; MTG$\to$PFC
(additionally same-study, donor-overlapping) and
ROSMAP$\leftrightarrow$MTG together bound the claim to
\emph{related-neuropathology} portability, not general biological
transfer.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
(i) Donor counts --- not cell counts --- bound statistical power; the
primary MTG comparisons leave CI widths of $\sim$0.5 in $\Delta\rho$,
so small true effects are not excluded. (ii) ROSMAP's public endpoint
is a coarse three-level ordinal, asymmetric with continuous CPS; the
two pathology tasks are related but not identical. (iii) MTG and PFC
share donors; the PFC leg is a robustness check. (iv) Text arms were
evaluated at one budget/architecture; a different text encoder or
longer training might differ, though the T1 equivalence makes large
gains unlikely under this endpoint. (v) Marker-recovery references are
coarse for fine brain subtypes, and the evaluation universe (2{,}000
HVGs) excludes most reference genes. (vi) Blood ran only in the
exploratory phase and has no pathology endpoint. (vii) The
pre-registered marker-recovery gate was infeasible by construction ---
a registration-time lesson: gate feasibility should be verified against
the evaluation space before registration, and we report the gate as
failed rather than silently rescoping it. (viii) The arm matrix
equalizes capacity and label access but not endpoint-supervision form;
a text arm with a B2-style endpoint head is the natural next experiment
and is deliberately out of the frozen protocol's scope here.

\paragraph{Reproducibility and availability.}
Code, fold manifests, run manifests, per-donor predictions,
permutation-null artifacts, gate JSONs and reports are versioned at
\url{https://github.com/alrobles/biocellAI} (release
\texttt{v0.1.0-alpha}, DOI \texttt{10.5281/zenodo.23103004}), with
derived artifacts on Hugging Face
(\texttt{alrobles/biocellai-foundations}). Controlled-access source data
(SEA-AD, ROSMAP raw) are not redistributed; fold contracts record
hashes sufficient to reconstruct them from the original repositories.
Paper figures and tables are generated from committed artifacts by
\texttt{scripts/v2\_paper\_figures.py} --- no result is hand-copied.

\section{Conclusion}

Under frozen data contracts, equal capacity and label access, and
donor-level inference with permutation controls, contrastive
biological-text alignment shows no detectable benefit over an equally
endpoint-blind, non-semantic reference --- in exploratory or
confirmatory phases, on either of two cohorts --- and falls short of
direct endpoint supervision. The same protocol confirms that
donor-level pathology signal is real and transfers across cohorts when
the predictor is strictly source-fitted, and decomposes how much
apparent signal the non-endpoint channels carry. The protocol --- not a
positive text-grounding claim --- is the durable result: it converts a
literature-level question into an evaluable experiment whose negative
outcomes are as reportable as positive ones.
